# Verified trajectories beat GRPO for Tiny Aya

Fine-tune **Tiny Aya Global**, and do not start with GRPO. Over ten days on 2×T4 with about $20-30 of credit, the best expected return comes from three stages. First, a correctly masked LoRA SFT on programmatic oracle trajectories from your own SQLite environment. Second, one or two rounds of **rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT)**: sample several rollouts per task, keep only the verified successes, and retrain. Third, only if the SFT/RFT policy produces a mix of successes and failures on roughly a fifth or more of training tasks, a short AgentTune GRPO or RLOO run on a rented bf16 L4. GRPO cannot learn from a policy that succeeds 0-2.5% of the time, because groups where every rollout fails have zero advantage and so give no gradient. Every small-model SQL or tool-use RL success in the evidence started from an SFT cold start, and the most controlled 2026 study found LoRA-SFT best in-distribution in 15 of 18 settings. Logit-level and on-policy distillation from Aya Expanse are ruled out: the two tokenizers share only 2 token IDs, and Aya Expanse 8B solves just 12.5% of your tasks. On data, your environment's generator is the primary training source. SynSQL-2.5M (Apache-2.0, 16,583 SQLite DBs in a 54.5 MB zip) is the best permissive addition and BIRD train-filtered (CC-BY-SA-4.0) the best human-written one. A fixed subset of BIRD mini-dev SQLite is the best external before/after benchmark. Most throughput and cost figures here are back-of-envelope estimates, not measurements. The most likely way to fail is not the choice of algorithm but a silent bug in stop tokens, loss masking or tokenizer IDs, so spend day one checking those.

## Tiny Aya Global is the only model you can afford to iterate on

All five Tiny Aya variants are the same **3.35B dense Cohere2 decoder**: 36 layers, 8K context, a 262k vocabulary, and 6T pretraining tokens that include code. Fire, Water and Earth are regional SFT checkpoints merged with Global. Their documented advantage is translation into their regional languages, **+1.7 to +5.5 chrF**, which does nothing for an English SQL agent ([Tiny Aya report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11510)). Global is the natural starting point because Cohere applied only "a minimal preference tuning phase", kept deliberately small "to facilitate users and researchers in adapting the model to downstream tasks" ([Tiny Aya report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11510)). Base would spend your small dataset relearning chat formatting and turn-ending. Skip the suggested zero-shot check of Fire as well: at a floor of 0-2.5%, a zero-shot comparison cannot tell the variants apart.

Compute settles the choice between families. Tiny Aya's fp16 weights are **6.70 GB** ([mirror file listing](https://huggingface.co/1-800-LLMs/tiny-aya-global/tree/main)). Aya Expanse 8B's are **16.06 GB** ([mirror file listing](https://huggingface.co/adamo1139/aya-expanse-8b-ungated/tree/main)), which does not fit one 16 GB T4 in fp16. Only Tiny Aya leaves room for both SFT and an RL loop on Kaggle hardware, with rollouts on one GPU and training on the other. Aya Expanse 8B is workable for 4-bit QLoRA SFT, while its GRPO would be tight and fragile. Aya Expanse 32B needs roughly 18-20 GB even at 4-bit by the notes' arithmetic, so training it here is out. There is also direct precedent. A Tiny Aya Global LoRA (rank 8, 240 demos, 60 steps, 349 seconds) was trained on a free Colab T4 *through Lexsi's AgentTune* and raised exact-correct tool actions from **2/24 to 19/24** on held-out cases. Its author warns that the benchmark is synthetic, single-step and not blinded, and unauthorized execute attempts rose from 0 to 3 ([ToolBoundary adapter](https://huggingface.co/Aditya0427/ToolBoundary-tiny-aya-lora)).

The case for Aya Expanse 8B deserves a hearing. It has the stronger reasoning prior: **MGSM 67.0** against Tiny Aya Global's **GlobalMGSM 52.8**, though the language sets differ so the comparison is only directional ([Aya Expanse report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04261); [Tiny Aya report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11510)). It also has a better measured baseline on your task, 12.5% against 0%. Tiny Aya trails Qwen3-4B-class peers by 2-10 points on math ([Tiny Aya report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11510)), and that weaker ceiling is the main risk on multi-hop questions. Aya Expanse's native `tool_use` template is no real advantage. It is the legacy Command-R format, with `parameter_definitions`, a `directly-answer` tool and no tool-result role ([Aya Expanse tokenizer config mirror](https://huggingface.co/adamo1139/aya-expanse-8b-ungated/blob/main/tokenizer_config.json)). Tiny Aya's template has no tool role at all ([Tiny Aya tokenizer config mirror](https://huggingface.co/1-800-LLMs/tiny-aya-global/blob/main/tokenizer_config.json)). So both families need the same custom protocol taught by SFT.

The practical rule follows from that. Build the SFT data once, in a model-agnostic message format. Switch to Aya Expanse 8B, with QLoRA SFT only, if Tiny Aya's post-SFT dev success plateaus clearly below what 8B reaches on the same data. Use Aya Expanse 32B only as a possible teacher you query, through the live "cohere" inference provider HF lists for it ([Hub repo details](https://hf.co/CohereLabs/aya-expanse-32b)), never as a model you train. Licences do not separate the options: all seven are **CC-BY-NC-4.0 and gated**. Publish a LoRA adapter under that licence that links to the gated base, as the ToolBoundary card does.

## Your own environment is the core dataset; public data buys transfer and a yardstick

The hackathon scores verified success on your own environment: 40 dev tasks and a sealed 97-task test that includes three held-out multi-hop families. Your task generator is therefore the primary training source. It produces unlimited tasks across 8 families on fresh database seeds, each with a reference-SQL oracle, which is a perfect in-environment teacher. Public data has two jobs. The first is protecting generalisation to the held-out families. AgentTuning found that "training solely on agent data, in fact, leads to a decline in generalization performance" ([AgentTuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12823)), and a 2026 controlled study of tool-calling SFT reports that dataset mixing gives strong transfer ([NeurIPS 2026 SLM-Agents poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/171173)). The second is an external benchmark judges will recognise.

Public multi-turn SQL *trajectories* barely exist, so the realistic move is to convert executable task sets into trajectories in your own `list_tables → describe_table → run_sql → ANSWER:` protocol. **SynSQL-2.5M** is the best source. It is Apache-2.0, "generated entirely using open-source LLMs", and ships **16,583 SQLite databases in a 54.5 MB `databases.zip`** next to 2.5M question/SQL pairs ([SynSQL-2.5M card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/seeklhy/SynSQL-2.5M)). **BIRD train-filtered** is the best human-written source: **6,601** quality-checked examples under CC-BY-SA-4.0. SFT on it lifted Qwen2.5-3B from **26.2 to 46.0 EX** on BIRD Mini-Dev. Its databases come from a separate Aliyun download whose size the notes did not verify ([bird23-train-filtered card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/birdsql/bird23-train-filtered)). **SkyRL-SQL-653** is a 653-task RL prompt pool with gold SQL, not a trajectory set. It has no licence tag, and its prompts embed the full CREATE TABLE schema, which you should strip so the agent has to explore ([SkyRL-SQL-653 data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NovaSky-AI/SkyRL-SQL-653-data)). **When2Call** (CC-BY-4.0, 15k SFT and 9k preference rows, 3,652-item MCQ test) targets hallucinated tool use directly ([When2Call card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/When2Call)). The **Toucan-1.5M** SFT subset (Apache-2.0, 119,287 rows from real MCP tool executions) is optional general tool hygiene ([Toucan card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Agent-Ark/Toucan-1.5M)). Both are low priority next to SQL data.

Several popular sets should be avoided. **APIGen-MT-5k** is CC-BY-NC-4.0, carries an OpenAI "should not be used to develop models that compete" clause, and covers τ-bench domains ([APIGen-MT-5k card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/APIGen-MT-5k)). **AgentInstruct's** 538-row DB split is GPT-4 output with no declared licence ([AgentInstruct card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zai-org/AgentInstruct)). The schema-in-context single-shot corpora (b-mc2/sql-create-context, Clinton/Text-to-sql-v1, WikiSQL, glaive) teach the model to answer from a pasted schema, which is exactly the "skip exploration" habit you are trying to remove. Clinton's card also has no description at all ([Clinton card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Clinton/Text-to-sql-v1)). Spider 2.0 and LiveSQLBench are too hard to be informative at 3B: strong 7B SQL specialists score 13-16% on Spider2.0-SQLite ([Arctic-Text2SQL-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20315v2)).

For evaluation, the sealed test stays primary. As the external number, use a fixed 100-200-question subset of **BIRD mini-dev SQLite**, run in your agent loop. The 2026 revision explicitly targets agents that "explore the permitted database", and it is hard: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B scores **21.2** EX on SQLite. The archived 2025-07 version has llama3-8b-instruct at **24.4** ([BIRD mini-dev card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/birdsql/bird_mini_dev); [archived card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/birdsql/bird_mini_dev/blob/main/archive/bird_mini_dev_2025-07-04/README.md)). The notes expect a Tiny Aya agent to land somewhere between single digits and the low 20s. Name the version you used and the scoring rule: restrict to single-value questions, or match the result set of the last successful `run_sql`. Spider dev is only a sanity check, since its test set has been public since Feb 2024 and Arctic-Text2SQL-R1 even trains on dev ([Spider site](https://yale-lily.github.io/spider); [Arctic paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20315v2)). Never train on BIRD dev or mini-dev databases. BIRD train databases are disjoint from them, which keeps the comparison clean.

Licences decide how you publish. BIRD and Spider derivatives must carry **CC-BY-SA-4.0** with attribution. Apache-2.0 and CC-BY-4.0 material (SynSQL, Toucan, When2Call) can be folded into a BY-SA release, while NC material cannot. Publish one dataset config per source licence and list each licence in the card. That is the notes' reading of CC compatibility, not legal advice. Trajectories *generated by Tiny Aya* during RFT raise a separate question under Cohere's CC-BY-NC terms that the notes did not resolve.

## GRPO from a near-zero base has no gradient to follow

### Why GRPO cannot be the first stage

GRPO's advantage is a reward minus its group mean, so a group where every rollout fails contributes nothing. At your base rate (pass@4 on 4 of 40 tasks), roughly nine in ten tasks would produce all-zero groups at G=4. AgentTune's own verified examples show the failure. Agentic-RAG GRPO on Qwen2.5-3B logged "`reward_std` 0.0, `frac_reward_zero_std` 1.0, and `loss` 0.0" ([AgentTune REAL_EXAMPLES](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune/blob/main/examples/REAL_EXAMPLES.md)). Without a cold start, a 4B tool agent suffered "interaction collapse": it stopped sustaining multi-turn tool use, and **~4K interaction-dense SFT trajectories** fixed it ([ASTER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01204)). A September 2026 study cites prior work finding pure RL "too unstable to bootstrap search behaviour from a cold start" at 0.5B-1B, and its own pure-RL success began from a non-zero floor ([small search agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28765v1)). SQL-Trail, the only multi-turn SQL RL paper with a 3B model, uses SFT on 1,000 distilled trajectories before RL on 1,027 questions. Its authors note that small models otherwise produce formatting errors, premature tool calls and loops ([SQL-Trail](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17699v1)). Every strong SQL-RL result in the notes (SkyRL-SQL, Arctic, SQL-R1, Progress-SQL) starts from Qwen2.5-Coder models that already score 50-80% EX.

Even when RL does work, it mostly sharpens what the model can already do. RLVR models beat their bases at small k but fall behind at large k, and their reasoning is "bounded by the base model", while distillation can introduce new patterns ([Yue et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837v4)). ProRL is the counter-evidence: prolonged RL on 136K problems produced genuinely new strategies at 1.5B ([ProRL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24864)). That needs compute orders of magnitude beyond yours. The 2026 controlled study of 0.6B-32B tool-calling agents found **LoRA-SFT best in-distribution in 15 of 18 settings**. GRPO won cross-dataset settings by under a point on average, and SFT followed by GRPO was rarely strongest ([NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/171173)). CacheRL reached a similar conclusion at 4B: "RL improves training stability but yields limited gains beyond strong SFT" ([CacheRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14179)).

### SFT plus rejection sampling captures most of the gain

Your observed failures are protocol failures: not ending the turn and fabricating tool results, alongside an 80-96% valid-call rate. That is format-following, which Agent-FLAN reports is learned faster than reasoning. Agent-FLAN also found that agent tuning can *increase* hallucination unless negative samples are added ([Agent-FLAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12881)). A few hundred verified trajectories is the normal scale. AgentLM-7B went from **0.3 to 30.6** on AgentBench-DB with a ~538-trajectory DB slice, built only from reward-1 trajectories ([AgentTuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12823)). Filtering matters more than volume. One practitioner saw SFT on *unfiltered* trajectories drop Qwen3-4B from 14.3% to 8.6%. That is a single unreviewed blog, but it agrees with AgentTuning's r=1 filter ([jbarnes850 blog](https://jbarnes850.github.io/2026/01/15/when-sampling-beats-training)).

RFT is the cheapest step after SFT when you have a verifier. Sample K rollouts per task, keep the verified successes, and retrain. On interactive SQL with Llama-2-7B, the ladder runs **SFT 54.9 → RFT 56.3 → ETO 57.2 → IPR 61.3** ([IPR](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11176v2)). Rejection samples pooled from several models took LLaMA-7B on GSM8K from 35.9% to 49.3% ([RFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)). The gains are modest and not guaranteed. In the Early Experience paper a STaR-style self-training baseline actually *underperformed* imitation learning (25.0 vs 47.3 on WebShop at 8B) ([Early Experience](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08558)). So gate each round on dev. Follow the ReST-EM convention of retraining from the base model each round ([ReST-EM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06585)), and stop after one or two rounds, since IPR overfit by round 5. Prefer the shortest verified episode per task, so that RFT also rewards efficiency.

The best-evidenced 2025-2026 addition is **Early Experience's self-reflection** variant (ICML 2026). The policy tries alternative actions at expert states, executes them, and learns from the real next states. Across 3B-8B models and eight environments, the notes' researcher averaged the paper's table to about **+6.5 points over imitation learning**, including +8.0 on BFCLv3 at Llama-3.2-3B ([Early Experience](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08558)). Your 66 injected-mistake episodes are already a hand-built version. Extending them programmatically costs no GPU time: wrong column, wrong table, SQL syntax error, empty result, the cents-versus-dollars trap, each followed by the *real* error message and a correct retry. Avoid the paper's other variant, implicit world modelling. It trains the model to predict observations, and predicting observations is exactly the fabrication failure you are removing.

### If you run RL, use these GRPO settings and nothing more exotic

The variants differ less than the debate suggests, and TRL exposes most of them as flags. The feature that matters at a low success rate is **dynamic sampling, or zero-variance filtering**, from DAPO and Magistral: drop prompts whose group is all-pass or all-fail ([HF agentic-RL 2026 overview](https://huggingface.co/blog/sergiopaniego/agentic-rl-2026)). You get it almost free by pre-filtering the task pool with per-task success rates logged during RFT sampling. Use TRL's default `loss_type="dapo"` (token-level loss plus clip-higher, with ε_high around 0.28). **Dr. GRPO**'s removal of length and std normalisation is a reasonable alternative. **GSPO, CISPO and GMPO** mainly target the stability of large and MoE models and add little for a 3B dense LoRA. **GiGPO** is conceptually ideal for you: every rollout of a task starts at the same state and repeats `list_tables()`, which gives natural "anchor states". It beat GRPO by more than 12% on ALFWorld and 9% on WebShop at 1.5B-7B ([GiGPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978v3)). But it lives only in verl-agent, and porting it to T4s in ten days is unrealistic. AgentTune also offers RLOO, which climbed slightly further than GRPO in its verified runs (0.031 → 0.703 vs 0.656 on SmolLM2-360M), while PPO "did not reliably climb" ([AgentTune REAL_EXAMPLES](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune/blob/main/examples/REAL_EXAMPLES.md)). Either is fine. Pick whichever survives the smoke test.

Keep the reward simple and gated. Arctic-Text2SQL-R1 used 1 for an execution match, 0.1 for executable SQL and 0 otherwise. It found that finer-grained rewards caused "lazy" behaviour, and that starting RL from an SFT checkpoint beat starting from base ([Arctic-Text2SQL-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20315)). SQL-Trail argues the other way for small models, weighting execution ×5 and adding turn-budget and bigram terms because binary execution is "overly sparse" ([SQL-Trail](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17699v1)). Pre-filtering the pool to mixed-outcome tasks largely resolves this disagreement, because sparsity stops mattering once every group has variance. Use +1 for a verified answer backed by at least one successful `run_sql` (your existing metric), a small +0.1 for a clean final `ANSWER:` line that counts only when the episode ends properly, a small per-turn penalty beyond about six turns, and zero on truncation. Fabrication is blocked structurally rather than by reward: stop at the call boundary, inject the real result, and mask tool tokens out of the loss, which AgentTune does with `env_mask` ([AgentTune](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune)). Multi-turn RL beats single-turn RL on the same data. SkyRL-SQL reports reaching 60% training reward **2.8× faster** ([secondary summary](https://machinelearningatscale.substack.com/p/text-to-sql-just-got-a-lot-better)), and SQL-Trail reports 59.3 vs 56.3. Watch reward standard deviation and entropy every step. In RAGEN's "Echo Trap" both fall *before* performance collapses ([RAGEN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20073)).

### Preference methods and distillation rank below RFT

Trajectory DPO works only with an NLL term on the chosen trajectory. IPR without its SFT loss **fell from 61.3 to 31.7** on InterCodeSQL ([IPR](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11176v2)). Early Experience reports that "DPO collapsed within tens of steps" ([Early Experience](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08558)), and multi-turn DPO carries a length bias when winning and losing trajectories differ in length ([DMPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14868)). KTO's unpaired desirable/undesirable labels fit binary verified rewards better ([KTO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306)). Two plumbing risks make either a fallback rather than a plan. AgentTune's `AuditReader.extract_dpo_pairs` always returns an empty list ([AgentTune known issues](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune/blob/main/docs/community/known-issues.md)). And nobody has verified whether TRL's DPOTrainer masks the folded tool-result turns inside multi-turn chosen/rejected completions. If it does not, DPO would train on observation text, which teaches fabrication.

On-policy distillation is the most talked-about new method. Thinking Machines report it at about **9-30× cheaper than SFT and ~10× cheaper than RL** ([Thinking Machines OPD](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)), but it needs the teacher to score the student's exact tokens. Tiny Aya and Aya Expanse have a vocabulary Jaccard of **0.316**, with only **2 shared strings at the same ID**, by the notes' own measurement from public mirrors ([Tiny Aya mirror](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/tiny-aya-global-8bit-mlx); [Aya Expanse mirror](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/aya-expanse-8b-4bit)). Cross-tokenizer GOLD recovered 60% of teacher performance at a token similarity of 0.64, nearly failed at 0.063, and needs ~1,000 steps before gains appear ([HF GOLD](https://huggingfaceh4-on-policy-distillation.hf.space/)). Alibaba's strict-position variant is promising, but it has been tested only on math and code ([arXiv 2610.08448](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.08448)). Above all, the best allowed teacher you have measured solves 12.5% of your tasks. Off-policy distillation is tokenizer-agnostic, but your programmatic oracle is already a perfect in-environment teacher. An Aya Expanse 32B harvest would add only natural-language diversity, so probe it on 20 dev tasks before spending anything. Using a non-Cohere teacher, as SQL-Trail did with Claude, raises rules and terms questions you can avoid entirely.

The heavyweight multi-turn frameworks are recipes to borrow, not stacks to adopt. verl's hardware table puts GRPO-LoRA for 3B on a single H100. SkyRL's examples request 4×48 GB GPUs ([verl hardware table](https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/perf/device_tuning.html); [SkyPilot SkyRL example](https://docs.skypilot.co/en/latest/examples/training/skyrl.html)). TRL's native `tools=` and `environment_factory` paths require a prefix-preserving, tool-aware chat template, and Cohere is not among the supported families ([TRL GRPO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/grpo_trainer)). AgentTune's auto-built `rollout_func`, which already handles Tiny Aya's fallback format, is therefore the route.

## Rollouts, not training, consume the budget

AgentTune can carry every stage, which matters if judges check that you "use AgentTune". `create_distill_trainer` runs LoRA SFT and accepts your own trainer via `trainer_factory`. `create_agentic_trainer(algorithm="grpo"|"rloo"|"dpo")` wraps TRL with multi-turn tool rollouts and `env_mask`. AuditKIT reads the resulting `trajectories.jsonl` through `episodes_from_agenttune` ([AgentTune README](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune)). Pass `backend="trl"` explicitly. The default `"auto"` silently switches to Unsloth if it can import it, and Unsloth patches model classes process-wide ([AgentTune backend selection](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune/blob/main/docs/getting-started/backend-selection.md)). AgentTune's Unsloth pin, `2026.8.19`, predates two silent-correctness fixes for exactly this model: rebuilding the tokenizer when "transformers v5 changes its ids" for tiny-aya (merged 2026-09-27), and applying Cohere's logit scale in the fused loss (merged 2026-09-18) ([Unsloth PR #12130](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/pull/12130); [Unsloth PR #11277](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/pull/11277)). No public AgentTune run on a Cohere model, a T4 or a SQL agent exists. Its verified runs are on SmolLM2-360M and Qwen2.5-3B, with the warning to "evaluate the saved adapter, because the in-memory model generates garbage" ([AgentTune REAL_EXAMPLES](https://github.com/Lexsi-Labs/AgentTune/blob/main/examples/REAL_EXAMPLES.md)).

T4s suit SFT and are weak for RL. They lack bf16 and FlashAttention 2. vLLM nominally supports them (compute capability ≥7.5) and lists `Cohere2ForCausalLM` with LoRA ([vLLM GPU install](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/getting_started/installation/gpu.html); [vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models.html)). Turing-specific kernel failures were reported as recently as v0.30.0rc1, though ([vLLM forum](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/title-outofresources-shared-memory-98304-65536-on-turing-sm75-global-attention-layers-with-head-dim-512/2990)). Unsloth also reported QLoRA GRPO diverging past about 60 steps on fp16-only GPUs such as the T4 ([Unsloth vision-RL blog](https://unsloth.ai/blog/vision-rl)). ## Conclusion

This project is less a question of which RL algorithm to use than of whether the plumbing is right. The baseline's signature failures (not ending the turn, fabricating observations) match what a missing stop token, a wrong template or an unmasked tool turn would produce, and a known Tiny Aya tokenizer-ID issue under transformers v5 sits directly in your dependency range. The jump from 0% will come almost entirely from SFT done correctly, and RL can add at most single digits on top. That suggests a different framing for the submission. Treat GRPO as a preregistered experiment with a kill criterion, not the headline. A clean "RL did not beat RFT" result fits the rigour-first stance you have already taken and costs a few dollars. RL that runs on an unverified pipeline risks the whole result.

The decisive measurement is cheap and comes free with RFT: the per-task success distribution under sampling after SFT. That one log decides whether RFT has headroom (pass@4 against pass@1), whether GRPO can get any gradient (the share of tasks with 0<p<1), and which tasks go into the GRPO pool, all with no extra compute. Your environment generates unlimited verifiable tasks, so data is not the scarce resource. Rollout throughput is: where you sample (laptop Ollama, L4 vLLM, or a T4 at batch 1) will shape what you can finish by 14 November far more than the choice between DAPO, Dr. GRPO and GSPO.
